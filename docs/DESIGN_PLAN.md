# Crossroads: The Silk Roads

## Product Thesis

The Silk Roads become understandable when their large systems are experienced through ordinary obligations. The player is not an emperor controlling Eurasia. The player leads one small merchant household whose survival depends on animals, guides, translators, local villagers, toll officials, brokers, and trust.

The first campaign follows Samira's caravan from Merv toward Bactra around AD 200. Later acts extend eastward through relay trade rather than sending the same party implausibly from Rome to China.

## Human Design Rules

- Every resource must answer a human question: who needs it and what happens without it?
- Every traveler has a private reason for taking the road.
- Decisions create reactions and memories, not just metric changes.
- Illness, animal care, debt, language, hospitality, and reputation drive play.
- Historical explanation follows the consequence; it never gives away the best answer first.
- Every choice receives a compact aftermath: reaction, exact changed measures, and the historical relationship the event demonstrates.
- Failure is an epilogue and lesson, not a sudden anonymous game-over screen.

## Campaign Shape

1. **Merv to Bactra:** household survival, credit, animal care, tolls, and local trust.
2. **Bactra Market:** sell, hold, or hand cargo to another merchant; recruit local partners.
3. **Across the Pamirs:** altitude, guides, seasonal closure, and fragmented authority.
4. **Kashgar Exchange:** languages, measures, religious communities, and commercial information.
5. **Dunhuang Relay:** conclude the household story and reveal how goods continue without their original owners.

## Network Layer

The continental map unlocks after the first arrival. Players invest limited profits in route relationships, caravanserai contracts, translators, warehouses, and credit. They influence a distributed network but never command every road. Goods, knowledge, disease, and political news use the same connections.

## Implemented Chapters

- **Chapter I: Merv to Bactra** contains six road encounters and an arrival reckoning.
- **Chapter II: The Bactra Exchange** contains customs, lodging, inherited credit, family settlement, and three distinct eastward handoff endings.
- **Chapter III: Across the Pamirs** follows the household-east branch through guide selection, seasonal crossings, altitude illness, highland hospitality, and the pass decision.
- **Chapter IV: Kashgar: Where Roads Divide** turns the mountain outcome into an oasis arrival, then follows water access, relay exchange, correspondence, religious transmission, and the choice between two eastern roads or a permanent relay house.
- **Chapter V: Dunhuang Relay** brings the two Tarim corridors back together at a Han commandery, where frontier papers, beacon warnings, state transport demands, and distant claims culminate in the household's final handoff.
- Household changes persist. A traveler who settles or returns to Bactra remains visible as a local partner rather than silently returning to the caravan, and later travel injuries no longer affect that person.

## Vertical Slice Acceptance

- A new player understands the immediate decision in under 30 seconds.
- The opening six-encounter journey takes 8-12 minutes; the complete eastbound route remains playable in short chapter sessions.
- Players remember at least two travelers by name after finishing.
- Players can explain that trade moved through regional relays.
- At least 60 percent voluntarily begin a second journey.
